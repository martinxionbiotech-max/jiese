CBT techniques are cognitive-behavioral skills: noticing the automatic thoughts that precede an urge, examining unhelpful beliefs, and rehearsing alternative responses. They are usually learned with a therapist, though some people practice simplified versions on their own.

Cognitive behavioral therapy has research support for treating compulsive sexual behaviors, which is why these techniques are labeled evidence-supported. Self-applied CBT-style skills have less direct evidence for problematic pornography use specifically, and learning them well usually benefits from professional guidance.
