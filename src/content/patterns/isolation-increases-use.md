This pattern describes a recurring observation that periods of isolation — living or spending long hours alone — appear repeatedly in reports of increased use.

This is correlational. Privacy removes barriers to use, and isolation is often accompanied by loneliness or low mood, which may themselves be the relevant factors. It is unclear whether isolation causes increased use or whether both follow from something else. Some members report that intentional, structured time alone was not associated with urges.
