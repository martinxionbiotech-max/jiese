Setting realistic goals matters because unrealistic goals — typically, a perfect streak from day one — tend to backfire when they break.

Members who describe more stable progress tend to set smaller, concrete goals about what they will do, rather than only what they will avoid. They also define a slip in advance, so that one mistake does not feel catastrophic, and they review and adjust their goals as they learn what works.

Goal-setting and self-monitoring have general evidence for behavior change, even if direct research on problematic pornography use is limited. The key is progress, not perfection.
